The Great Well. The Pond. Lake Mundi. It is known by many names, but 
this body of water has been the driving force that shapes society on 
the continent of Gol. Divided colloquially into three ‘rings’, most 
people don’t dare to venture past the first ring that hugs the 
coastline of the massive lake. Trading ships hug the coastline to 
avoid the massive storms that rage within the second ring, and 
because of this, have to contend with everyone’s favorite fantasy 
addition: pirates. Annoying, yes, but a far better option than having 
to contend with supercharged magical storms. Also, to make one thing 
very clear: nobody has ever been to the innermost ring and came back 
alive to tell the tale.
Lake Mundi isn’t the only fascinating aspect 
of the continent of Gol. While there aren’t gods in the traditional 
sense, Gol is the material facing side of the world of spirits. 
Through areas known as the ‘near-spirit’, or places where the ‘veil
’ is thin, spirits have been known to visit the material world and 
even interact with mortals. Every culture has differing views on 
spirits – some worship them as gods, some revile them as ghouls 
(there certainly are some ghoulish spirits), and some just appreciate 
them as another wonderful creation to behold in Gol. 
The final thing 
to mention in this introduction to the land of Gol is a little bit of 
important history. I won’t dive too deeply into it all, as it spans 
back generations, but I’d like to talk about the Citadel and the 
Krieger Imperium. Around 200 years ago, Kriega was a small nation 
ruled by the noble Krieger family. The Cloud Mountains and the Desert 
Bluum separated them from the 12 Houses that make up the land of 
Maltraav, but that didn’t stop certain Maltraavian houses from 
ransacking border towns of Kriega. In addition to that, the sorcerers 
of the Maltraavian houses and the people of Kriega had very different 
views on magic, culture, and humanity in general. War erupted. One 
member of the Krieger family, [name omitted, forgotten by history] 
Krieger, more famously known as the Wizard Strong, returned from the 
Citadel to his homeland, and explained that the Citadel and Kriega 
are hereby allies, and the Citadel will lend its awe-inspiring power 
to the war. The Citadel is a city, an organization, an academy, but 
really, it is an idea. Anybody who desires to learn the Lengua 
Arcana, or magic through wizardry, aspires to study and live in the 
Citadel, and to eventually earn their name-cloak. The Citadel is a 
powerhouse, and it always has been. Before this war, however, they 
never meddled in political affairs. They kept to themselves. This is 
perhaps the most influential turning point in history, the moment the 
Citadel decided to ally themselves with the Kriega. How did this 
happen? Why? It is still unclear to this day. But because of their 
intervention, Kriega pushed back the forces of the Maltraavianhouses 
back to their homelands, where they (mostly) remain to this day. In 
the last 200 years, skirmishes have popped up here and there, but it’
s mostly been a cold war between the Maltraavian houses and Kriega. 
If only the 12 Houses would all band together…
After their victory 
200 years ago, Kriega had a newfound confidence, mostly because of 
their powerful allies. Facing very little opposition, they expanded, 
seizing control of most of the countries that surround Lake Mundi. 
The marsh tribes of the Dagger Swamp, the Scoia’tael, and a group of 
hardy dwarven sailors of the Free Islands (led by the Kraken Clan) 
were able to resist, but the rest of the people of Gol found 
themselves to suddenly be citizens of the Krieger Imperium. This isn’
t inherently a bad thing, as the Imperium has brought 
industrialization, opportunity, and a booming economy to the 
conquered lands. Quality of life has greatly increased for many 
members of the Imperium. Even if it wasn’t by choice.
